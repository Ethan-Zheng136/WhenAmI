**The standard criticism, and who owns each strand.** There is no single "MINE is not a good estimator" paper — the critique is distributed across four results:

1. **Exponential variance.** Song & Ermon (1910.06222) prove estimators like MINE have variance that **grows exponentially in the true MI**, and show empirically that existing estimators violate basic self-consistency properties of MI (data processing, additivity under independence). This is the sharpest technical indictment.
2. **Sample-complexity impossibility.** McAllester & Stratos (1811.04251) prove that **any** distribution-free high-confidence lower bound on MI from N samples cannot exceed O(ln N) — so all these estimators are fundamentally capped by sample size, not just badly tuned.
3. **The log-batch-size ceiling.** InfoNCE/CPC is bounded above by log(batch size); Poole et al. (1905.06922) lay out the bias-variance tradeoff across the bound family (NWJ/DV low-bias-high-variance vs. InfoNCE low-variance-high-bias) and show none escape it.
4. **MI isn't even the causal ingredient.** Tschannen et al. (1907.13625) show downstream success in InfoMax-style methods often **cannot be attributed to MI at all** — tighter bounds can yield *worse* representations, and gains track encoder inductive bias and the critic's parametric form instead. This is the most damaging for anyone using an MI estimate as a headline metric.

**Practical upshot for a metric:** a raw neural MI number is hard to defend as a quantitative claim. Use it for ranking/relative comparison, or move to a computationally-bounded notion (V-information, §3) where the estimand is well-posed by construction.

### The KEY target: "information in Y about T already explained by Z"

**This exists, and the direct hit is from NLP probing, not the IB or PID literature:**

**Conditional probing: measuring usable information beyond a baseline** — [2109.09234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.09234), 2021-09-19, Hewitt, Ethayarajh, Liang, Manning (Stanford NLP). Comments: `EMNLP 2021 + typo fixes`. **Nonlinear (probe-family-bounded); conditioning is the entire point.** This is precisely your quantity: how much a representation Y tells you about target T *beyond* what baseline Z already provides, formalized as conditional V-information I_V(Y→T | Z). It also empirically demonstrates the failure mode motivating the whole idea — unconditional probing ranks a representation highly for information the baseline already had. Foundation: **A Theory of Usable Information Under Computational Constraints**, [2002.10689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10689), Xu, Zhao, Song, Stewart, Ermon, `ICLR 2020 (Talk)` — V-information is what makes "information in a deterministic net" non-vacuous.

**Critical warning for your metric design.** No PID paper defines redundancy as I(T;Y) − I(T;Y|Z). That difference is **interaction information**, which **can be negative** and is not a PID redundancy term — it picks up synergy. Dutta et al., *Fairness Under Feature Exemptions* ([2006.07986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07986), Comments: `Accepted at the IEEE Transactions on Information Theory (Shorter version at AAAI 2020 as an oral presentation)`) argues explicitly that raw I(T;Y|Z) **overcounts** and that unique information is the correct measure of "disparity not explained by the side variable." The two defensible framings are **conditional V-information** (cite Hewitt 2109.09234) or **BROJA unique information** (cite 1311.2852 + estimator 2102.00218). Also note **Multivariate Dependence Beyond Shannon Information** ([1609.01233](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01233), James & Crutchfield, Comments: `10 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables; this http URL`) — a negative result showing PID cannot distinguish distributions with identical Shannon structure.

Not on arXiv: Tax/Mediano/Shanahan, *The PID of Generative Neural Network Models*, Entropy 19(9):474 (DOI 10.3390/e19090474); Dutta et al. PID-in-fairness survey, Entropy 25(5):795.
