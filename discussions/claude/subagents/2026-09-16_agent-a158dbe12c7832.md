## 🔴 HEADLINE FINDING — the diagnostic you are looking for

### The single closest paper to "how much of the auxiliary target is redundant given the current observation"

**Present but Not Remembered: Auditing How Frozen VLAs Encode, Deploy, and Steer Visual History**
- arXiv: **2607.03372** · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03372 · 2026-07-03
- Authors: Chih-Ting Liao, Xin Cao · Venue: no Comments field (preprint, cs.CV)
- **This is the redundancy diagnostic — but on the HISTORY axis, not the FUTURE axis.** Method: layer-resolved linear probing + causal interchange interventions across three frozen VLAs from two architecture families. It explicitly separates "decodable content" from "information unique beyond the current frame."
- **Verbatim finding:** *"Information unique to history beyond the current frame is nearly absent, indicating that stored history is largely a redundant copy of the present."* And: *"VLAs do not forget the past; they largely fail to represent it as information distinct from the present."*
- Prescription generalizes directly to your question: *"future memory augmentation should inject information unique to the past rather than simply more history."*
- **Why it matters for you:** this is the exact methodology (probe for present-irreducible information; contrast with a same-frame baseline) that the future-prediction literature does NOT apply to its own targets. The paper also notes its Bridge evaluation pairs are themselves **near-Markov** — i.e., it independently observes the low information-gain property of robot data. **Time-reverse this paper and you have the missing future-side diagnostic.**

### 🔴 The closest FUTURE-side diagnostic

**Beyond Task Success: Behavioral and Representational Diagnostics for WAM and VLA**
- arXiv: **2606.01095** · https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01095 · 2026-05-31
- Authors: Hung Mai, Bin Zhu, Tuan Do · Preprint (no Comments)
- **Asks your exact question in the abstract:** *"it remains unclear whether future prediction in WAMs leads to behaviorally meaningful improvements beyond final task success... we ask whether WAMs merely add future prediction, or whether they change robot behavior and internal representations in ways that are actionable for control."*
- Method: model-agnostic framework = behavioral rollout analysis (action dynamics consistency, target-object progress, distractor disturbance, runtime) + **sparse-autoencoder feature analysis classifying features as memorized / reactive / predictive.** 7 policies on LIBERO + RoboTwin2.0, spanning direct VLA and joint / sequential / **auxiliary** WAMs.
- **DIAGNOSES the gain: yes.** Key result: *"Sequential WAMs show the clearest predictive structure, while auxiliary and joint WAMs respectively compress or entangle future information."* Auxiliary WAMs (the head-on-a-VLA design) remain nearly indistinguishable from a plain VLA in feature space, and direct VLA baselines show an extremely low predictive-feature rate. Their framing: *"the key question is not whether a model predicts the future, but whether its future representation is actionable."*
- **Caveat vs. your need:** it classifies features as predictive/reactive but does NOT compute an information-gain quantity like I(z_future ; · | o_t) or a same-frame-copy control.

**Emergent World Representations in OpenVLA**
- arXiv: **2509.24559** · https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24559 · 2025-09-29
- Authors: Marco Molinari, Leonardo Nevali, Saharsha Navani, Omar G. Younis · Preprint
- **The "the student's own representation already contains the target" result.** Probes whether OpenVLA — trained with NO future-prediction objective — already encodes state transitions. Uses embedding arithmetic (difference of sequential state embeddings) + linear/nonlinear probes across layers, with an embedding baseline as control. Finds statistically significant transition recoverability exceeding baseline, emerging in middle layers; linear probes beat MLPs; predictive ability improves with longer horizons.
- **This is the strongest existing evidence that a future-prediction auxiliary may be teaching a VLA something it already has.** But the paper frames it positively ("world models emerge!"), not as a redundancy critique, and never converts it into an information-gain measurement against the auxiliary loss.

**Representation-Aligned Tactile Grounding for Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation**
- arXiv: **2607.14609** · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14609 · 2026-07-16
- Fudan et al. (Ruilin Chen et al.) · Preprint
- **Methodologically the cleanest "how predictable is the future target from which representation" study**, just on tactile rather than visual futures. Uses **frozen linear probes on raw future tactile sequences** to measure where in the VLA the future target is most linearly predictable — finds intermediate action-expert features beat both VLM-side features and final action outputs, and that **future-tactile predictability is non-monotonic along the action path.** Then places the auxiliary loss where the probe says. The probe-first protocol is directly transplantable to future-image/latent targets.

**What Do Latent Action Models Actually Learn?**
- arXiv: **2506.15691** · https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15691 · 2025-05-27 · **Comments: Accepted by NeurIPS-25**
- Microsoft Research (Chuheng Zhang, Tim Pearce, Li Zhao, Jiang Bian et al.)
- **The information-content measurement you asked for, on the latent-action target.** Poses the objective in mutual-information terms: the latent should maximize MI with the true action while **minimizing MI with the exogenous noise AND with the observation.** Because MI is intractable in general, they build a tractable **linear LAM** where it can be approximated, then freeze it and fit three linear readouts predicting action / exogenous noise / observation from the latent. Analytical result: linear LAM reduces to **PCA on a mixture of controllable change and exogenous noise**, which implies LAMs only work when the action-driven observation change dominates.
- **DIAGNOSES the gain: yes, this is the best-in-class instance.** It explicitly notes it remains unclear whether the information bottleneck actually eliminates the latent's dependence on the observation — i.e., the redundancy question, stated openly and left open.

**Reconstructing Is Not Acting: Action-Centric Latent Dynamics Modeling (ACT-LAM)**
- arXiv: **2609.15189** · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15189 · 2026-09-14 · Comments: Tech report, 26 pages
- **Names the failure mode outright: "reconstruction-action mismatch" — lower reconstruction error does not necessarily yield better latent dynamics or downstream performance.** Attributes it to two things, the second of which is your redundancy point verbatim: *"the FDM can underutilize the inferred latent action by exploiting predictive shortcuts from the current state."*
- Proposes a **counterfactual utilization metric**: compare rollouts driven by real latent actions vs. a constant action at every step and measure state-MSE reduction. This is effectively "how much does the auxiliary target add beyond what the current state already gives you" — the closest thing to a trivial-baseline control anywhere in this literature.
