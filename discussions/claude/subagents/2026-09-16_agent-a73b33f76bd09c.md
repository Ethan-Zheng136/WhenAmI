### A3. 2608.29998 — "The Intervention Gap in Latent World Models"
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998 — **VERIFIED**
- **v1:** Sun, 30 Aug 2026 19:47:51 UTC (177 KB). citation_date 2026/08/30.
- **Author / institution:** Donna Vakalis — **Mila – Quebec AI Institute** and **University of Montreal**. Single author.
- **Comments (raw):** `21 pages, 10 figures`
- **Subjects:** Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- **One-liner:** Defines **planning-time intervention fidelity** (does the model's own open-loop transition move task variables the way a *matched environment intervention* does?) and shows it is neither revealed by reward fit nor produced by task-anchored training; models can capture the current state and resolve *real* intervention effects while their *imagined* effects are **worse than predicting no effect at all**.

**Core numbers (VERIFIED):**
- **TD-MPC2 size sweep, cheetah-run, 5 released sizes:** return vs operator error rank correlation **−0.90** (anchor-bootstrap 95% CI [−0.90, −0.70]; LOO ≥ −0.80); partial rank correlation controlling for reward accuracy **−0.89**. Reward-prediction error **flat within [0.03, 0.09]**. The 317M checkpoint: "**near-best reward fit, worst operator**," collapsed return.
- Operator-error ranking vs Bellman-residual ranking: rank corr **+0.30** [+0.30,+0.60]; a *value-restricted* operator variant vs the same Bellman residual: **+1.00**. Interpretation: the extra signal lives entirely in task-observable directions the value channel does not constrain.
- **Task anchoring does not help:** three self-supervised LeWorldModel seeds (no task signal) had operator error **0.384 ± 0.009** vs task-anchored TD-MPC2 (single-task 0.840, multi-task 0.833); error ratio **0.457 ± 0.011**, disjoint 95% intervals across all three seeds. Restricting to shared well-probed observables *widens* the gap (ratio 0.395 ± 0.012). MLP probe instead of ridge: 0.495 ± 0.006.
- **THE PRIMARY DISSOCIATION (Cheetah, 3 LeWorldModel checkpoints, 5-step physical horizon, target = endpoint torso speed, thresholds fixed on 48 calibration families, scored on 96):** all three **pass** current-query capture and real-effect resolvability, then their imagined effects fail both confirmatory comparisons — "**Predicted-effect R² is far below zero, worse than predicting no effect at all**, with every whole-family 95% interval below zero, and every checkpoint's imagined endpoint is reliably worse than the environment-endpoint oracle."
- **The geometry of the failure (this is the mechanistic gold):** "the complete predicted feature change remains strongly aligned with the real one (**contrast cosine 0.85–0.89** across the three checkpoints), while the same change projected onto the task readout is **nearly orthogonal** to the real task effect and **1.7–2.6 times too large**." Named: **task-direction rotation with excess gain, not feature collapse**.
- Why generic metrics miss it: "This is why a generic feature-space rollout error would have looked benign here, and why trajectory-plausibility scores can miss the failure entirely."
- **Practice results:** In DreamerV3 "the categorical posterior distribution is the sole tested surface that supports the current query across checkpoints; its sample, its mode, and the deterministic state **all fail**." Ensemble disagreement ranks error only near training support; a frozen support-aware score **degrades** held-out error ranking in *both* transfer directions.
- **Boundary / non-replication:** five PreJEPA seeds retain an oracle-relative deficit *without* reproducing the severe conjunction; Finger Spin extends the deficit with heterogeneous severity across seeds.

**Verbatim limitations (§8 Scope and Limitations):**
> "The learned-model evidence covers Cheetah locomotion and Finger Spin, each with one primary scalar query and a five-step physical horizon. An eligibility survey examined 32 systems spanning 13 plausible families; only the five-run PreJEPA family passed every original primary criterion. Released TD-MPC2 sizes and some LeWorldModel checkpoints remain dependent descriptive panels. **The evidence therefore supports neither cross-family inference nor a population-level architecture ranking.** A Walker continuation is authorized but not yet terminal, and cannot be pooled with Cheetah or Finger."

> "The Section 3 results are **correlational and single-environment**. The size-sweep association uses five dependent released checkpoints from one training recipe; **it is a flag, not a selection rule.**"

> "Capture is relative to the fixed readout family and does not establish absence or availability under every nonlinear decoder. **Probe success also does not show that a planner uses the decoded information** (Elazar et al., 2021). Propagation fidelity need not imply high return when planning is discontinuous or action margins are small; **the experiments provide no checkpoint-selection result, planning-return improvement, or return guarantee.**"

> "The support-reference analyses remain correlational. Endpoint contraction, motion toward training support, and disagreement–error association **do not identify a causal mechanism**, and the geometric characterizations of Sections 4.2 and 5 (rotation with excess gain in one family, attenuation in another) constrain candidate mechanisms without selecting one. ... the Dreamer studies establish representation boundaries rather than a propagation result, so **Dreamer propagation remains uncertified.**"

**Explicit open problems (§9 Conclusion):**
> "**Two questions are deliberately left open**: which mechanism produces rotation with excess gain in one family and attenuation in another, and whether the audit's rankings carry decision value for checkpoint selection and return. Both require experiments this framework now makes well-posed."

**Relevance: MAXIMAL, and this is the one closest to a *counterfactual/interventional* framing.** It is literally an intervention-fidelity audit with matched do()-style environment interventions. The "rotation with excess gain, contrast cosine 0.85–0.89 but task projection near-orthogonal and 1.7–2.6× too large" result is the precise mechanism a C(z,a) formulation would need to beat — and note the paper explicitly leaves *why* open.
