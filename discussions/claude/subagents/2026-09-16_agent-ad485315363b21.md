# PID and "Redundancy Relative to Side Information" — Literature Report

## 1. PID Foundations

### Nonnegative Decomposition of Multivariate Information — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **1004.2515** | https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2515 | 2010-04-14
- Williams & Beer (Indiana University Bloomington)
- Comments (exact): `14 pages, 9 figures` — no venue stated
- (a) Nonlinear/discrete — lattice of "redundancy" measured by $I_{\min}$ over specificity; distribution-based, not linear. (b) No native conditioning on a third variable; the lattice is over *sources*, and any Z must be inserted as an extra source, which changes the lattice rather than conditioning.

### Quantifying unique information — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **1311.2852** | https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2852 | 2013-11-12
- Bertschinger, Rauh, Olbrich, Jost, Ay (MPI for Mathematics in the Sciences, Leipzig / Frankfurt Institute for Advanced Studies)
- Comments (exact): `24 pages, 2 figures. Version 2 contains less typos than version 1`
- Journal ref: `Entropy, 16 (2014) 4, p. 2161-2183` — matches the "Entropy 2014" you expected
- Note: exact title is lowercase "unique information", not title-cased.
- (a) Nonlinear — convex optimization (BROJA) over the set of joints sharing the pairwise marginals $p(Y,X_1), p(Y,X_2)$; a decision-theoretic operationalization. (b) Yes, in effect: $UI(Y : X_1 \setminus X_2)$ **is** a "unique info of $X_1$ about $Y$ given $X_2$", which is the closest classical match to your target quantity. Crucially it depends only on the two pairwise marginals, deliberately *not* the full joint — this is the axiom that makes it differ from $I(T;Y|Z)$.

### A Bivariate Measure of Redundant Information — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **1207.2080** | https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2080 | 2012-07-09
- Harder, Salge, Polani (University of Hertfordshire)
- Comments (exact): `16 pages, 15 figures, 1 table, added citation to Griffith et al 2012, Maurer et al 1999`
- Published in Phys. Rev. E 87, 012130 (2013), not stated in Comments.
- (a) Nonlinear — projective/geometric: redundancy defined via information projection onto the convex hull of conditional distributions ($I_{\text{proj}}$). (b) Bivariate only (two sources); no general third-variable conditioning.

### Quantifying synergistic mutual information — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **1205.4265** | https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.4265 | 2012-05-18
- Griffith & Koch (Caltech / Allen Institute)
- Comments (exact): `15 pages; 12 page appendix. Lots of figures. Guided Self Organization: Inception. Ed: Mikhail Prokopenko. (2014); ISBN 978-3-642-53734-9`
- (a) Nonlinear — synergy via constrained optimization ($S_{VK}$, "whole minus best part"). (b) No.

### Multivariate Dependence Beyond Shannon Information — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **1609.01233** | https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01233 | 2016-09-05
- James & Crutchfield (UC Davis, Complexity Sciences Center)
- Comments (exact): `10 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables; this http URL` (link to csc.ucdavis.edu)
- (a) Nonlinear/discrete; this is a *negative* result — argues PID quantities cannot distinguish distributions with identical Shannon structure but different dependency structure. Important caveat for anyone building a PID-based metric. (b) N/A.

### Pointwise Partial Information Decomposition using the Specificity and Ambiguity Lattices — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **1801.09010** | https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.09010 | 2018-01-27
- Finn & Lizier (University of Sydney)
- Comments (exact): `31 pages, 10 figures. (v1: preprint; v2: as accepted; v3: title corrected)`
- Journal ref: `Entropy, 20(4), 297, 2018`
- Note: full title is longer than "Pointwise Partial Information Decomposition" — v3 corrected the title.
- (a) Nonlinear — pointwise (local) decomposition splitting local MI into specificity and ambiguity, giving a two-lattice structure. (b) No.

### A Novel Approach to the Partial Information Decomposition — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **1908.08642** | https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08642 | 2019-08-23, last revised 2022-03-12 (v4)
- Kolchinsky (sole author; Universitat Pompeu Fabra / Santa Fe Institute at time of writing)

(b) Partially — the framework is parameterized by the class of allowed distributions, so a Z-conditioned instantiation is expressible, but the paper does not develop one.

### Estimating the Unique Information of Continuous Variables — **VERIFIED** (bonus; you need this to actually compute)
- arXiv: **2102.00218** | https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00218 | 2021-01-30

## 2. PID Applied to Deep Learning Representations

### Quantifying & Modeling Multimodal Interactions: An Information Decomposition Framework — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **2302.12247** | https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12247 | 2023-02-23
- Liang, Cheng, Fan, Ling, Nie, Chen, Deng, Allen, Auerbach, Mahmood, Salakhutdinov, Morency (CMU, with Brown / Columbia / Harvard Medical)

This is the closest existing *deep-learning* analogue of your target, with modality 2 playing the role of side information Z.

### Factorized Contrastive Learning: Going Beyond Multi-view Redundancy — **VERIFIED**
- arXiv: **2306.05268** | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05268 | 2023-06-08

**Assessment for your item 3:** no paper defines redundancy as "$I(T;Y)-I(T;Y|Z)$" under that name in the PID literature. Two distinct traditions converge on your quantity from opposite directions: (i) probing/$\mathcal{V}$-information (2109.09234) does the conditional-on-baseline computation but calls it "usable information beyond a baseline", uses a bounded probe family, and is not framed as PID; (ii) PID/unique-information (1311.2852, 2411.07483, 2006.07986) formalizes "what Y adds about T that Z does not" as $UI$ and *explicitly argues against* the raw $I(T;Y|Z)$ form because conditional MI can exceed unique information (it picks up synergy). If you intend to use $I(T;Y) - I(T;Y|Z)$ as "redundancy", expect a reviewer to cite 2006.07986 / 1311.2852 at you: that difference is the *interaction information*, which can be negative, and is not a PID redundancy term. The defensible framings are either conditional $\mathcal{V}$-information (cite Hewitt) or BROJA unique information (cite Bertschinger + Pakman's estimator).
