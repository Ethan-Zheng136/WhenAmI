## 2. Distillation as mutual information

**Variational Information Distillation for Knowledge Transfer** — 1904.05835 · 11 Apr 2019 · Ahn, Hu, Damianou, Lawrence, Dai (Amazon Cambridge) · **Comments: "To appear at CVPR 2019"** · **VERIFIED**

**This is the paper that is structurally closest to "predictability of the target from the student's representation."** Its exact formulation:
```
L = L_S − Σ_k λ_k I(t^(k); s^(k))                                    (2)
I(t;s) = H(t) − H(t|s) ≥ H(t) + E_{t,s}[log q(t|s)]                  (3)
L̃ = L_S − Σ_k λ_k E[log q(t^(k) | s^(k))]                            (4)
```
`q(t|s)` is a variational decoder predicting the **teacher's activations from the student's activations**. Because `H(t)` is constant w.r.t. student parameters, **the trainable objective is exactly a log-likelihood of predicting the target from the student representation** — i.e. VID's loss *is* a predictability measure, used as a training signal rather than a diagnostic. The paper says it "allows us to quantify the amount of information that is transferred from a teacher network to a student network."

**Does anyone frame "distillation gain" as an MI quantity?** Yes, and the sharpest is:
- **Information-Theoretic Criteria for KD in Multimodal Learning** — 2510.13182 · 15 Oct 2025 · Xie, Xu, Sanguinetti (SISSA/EPFL) · Comments: NONE · **VERIFIED**. The **Cross-modal Complementarity Hypothesis**: KD is effective iff `I(teacher_repr ; student_repr) > I(student_repr ; labels)`. Validated analytically in a joint Gaussian model plus empirically across image/text/video/audio/omics. This is the nearest existing thing to a *decision rule* for whether distillation will pay off.
- **On the Generalization of KD: An Information-Theoretic View** — 2605.13143 · 13 May 2026 (v2 15 May) · Li & He · Comments: "18 pages" · **VERIFIED**. Defines **distillation divergence** = KL between teacher and student *stochastic kernels*; derives upper (sub-Gaussian/stability) and lower (central condition) bounds; in a linear-Gaussian case the divergence decomposes into **bias + variance + rank-bottleneck** costs.
- **Estimating and Maximizing Mutual Information for KD (MIMKD)** — 2110.15946 · 29 Oct 2021 (v3 11 May 2023) · Shrivastava, Qi, Ordonez (UVA/Rice) · Comments: NONE · **VERIFIED**. JSD-based MI lower bound (stable with a single negative, unlike infoNCE); ResNet-34→18 ImageNet 70.32%.

## 3. Auxiliary-task redundancy — YES, predictability-from-frozen-representation IS the standard measure

**⭐ Taskonomy: Disentangling Task Transfer Learning** — 1804.08328 · 23 Apr 2018 · Zamir, Sax, Shen, Guibas, Malik, Savarese (Stanford/Berkeley) · **Comments: "CVPR 2018 (Oral)"** · **VERIFIED**. The origin of the measure, in their own words: affinity is computed "based on whether the solution for one task can be **sufficiently easily read out of the representation trained for another task**" — a small **readout function** trained on the source task's **frozen encoder**, raw losses normalized via AHP, transfer policy via Binary Integer Program. Framed explicitly as "a principled way for identifying **redundancies** across tasks."

**⭐ A Theory of Usable Information Under Computational Constraints** — 2002.10689 · **Comments: "ICLR 2020 (Talk)"** · **VERIFIED**. **Predictive V-information** — the theory that makes probe-based diagnostics rigorous. Two load-bearing properties: it **subsumes both MI and R²** as special cases (so a linear probe's R² *is* a V-information estimate), and it **violates the data processing inequality** (so computation can create usable information — which is why probing a *learned* representation is not vacuous).

**⭐ Conditional probing: measuring usable information beyond a baseline** — 2109.09234 · **Comments: "EMNLP 2021 + typo fixes"** · **VERIFIED**. Defines `I_V(φ(X) → Y | B)` — usable information **not already in baseline B** — and proves the naive `Perf(φ(X)) − Perf(B)` subtraction is *wrong* when the two capture distinct aspects of Y. **Set B = main-task representation, Y = auxiliary target and this is a first-principles auxiliary-redundancy score.**

**⭐ Negative Conditional Entropy** — 1908.08142 *Transferability and Hardness of Supervised Classification Tasks* · **Comments: "Published at ICCV 2019"** · **VERIFIED**. `−H(Y_target | Y_source)` on **labels only, no trained model at all**. Validated on 437 tasks. The cheapest possible redundancy pre-check when both targets exist on the same inputs.

Also verified: 2110.08420 *V-Usable Information* (**"ICML 2022 (Outstanding Paper)"**, adds pointwise PVI), 1904.11740 *RSA for Task Taxonomy* (**"Accepted at CVPR 2019"** — RDM correlation, no readout training), 2002.12462 *LEEP* (**"Published at ICML 2020"** — one forward pass, beats NCE and H-score), 2102.11005 *LogME* (**"ICML 2021 camera ready"** — Bayesian marginal likelihood of labels given frozen features; the probe *without* the capacity confound), 1902.03545 *Task2Vec* (Comments empty; ICCV 2019, Fisher diagonal of a frozen probe net), 2106.09362 *TransRate* (**"Accepted by ICML 2022"** — MI via coding rate), 2307.00823 *Transferability via Task-Relatedness* (**"NeurIPS 2024"**), 2003.12298 *MDL Probing* (Comments empty; EMNLP 2020), 2103.03872 *Rissanen Data Analysis* (ICML 2021), 2402.15231 transferability-estimation survey.

**Two guardrails, both already published against a naive probe:**
1. **Amnesic Probing** — 2006.00995 · **Comments: "TACL journal…"** · VERIFIED: *"conventional probing performance is **not correlated to task importance**."* Readable ≠ used. Pair any probe with causal removal (fix: 2506.11673, LEACE/mean projection).
2. **MDL Probing** — 2003.12298: plain probe accuracy "[does] not substantially favour pretrained representations over randomly initialized ones" and is similar for real vs random labels. Use codelength or LogME evidence, not raw accuracy.

**Gradient-side measures** (the auxiliary-task literature's dominant idiom, and it barely cites the probing literature): 2001.06782 PCGrad (**"NeurIPS 2020"** — negative gradient cosine); 1812.02224 *Adapting Auxiliary Losses Using Gradient Similarity* (Comments **empty** — **preprint / NeurIPS 2018 CL workshop only, not ICLR 2019**; best one-liner for the topic: "it is not always trivial to know if an auxiliary task will be helpful … and when it could start hurting"); 2108.11346 *Auxiliary Task Update Decomposition: The Good, The Bad and The Neutral* (**"Accepted to ICLR 2021"** — decomposes the auxiliary gradient into helping/damaging/**neutral** subspaces; the "neutral" subspace *is* the redundant component; subsumes PCGrad as a special case); 2109.04617 TAG (**"In NeurIPS 2021 (spotlight)"** — inter-task affinity as **lookahead loss ratio** `Z_{i→j} = 1 − L_j(θ − α∇L_i)/L_j(θ)`, from a *single* multi-task run); its unpublished predecessor 2010.15413 (**preprint only**); 2109.05748 GradTS (**"In EMNLP 2021"**).
